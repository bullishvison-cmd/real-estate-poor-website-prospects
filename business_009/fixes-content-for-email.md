What this redesign fixes 
 A straight comparison: what is missing on the brokerage's current website, why that costs enquiries from its own clients, and how this concept fixes it. No sales figures are claimed — none were available. 

 1 What&#x27;s missing on the real site No listings search or filter, and no actual listings anywhere on the site — the homepage is generic text sections only. The gallery is stock Pexels photos (a kitchen, a hotel room, living rooms), not real listings. A template error says &#x27;with Apex, a trusted real estate professional&#x27; — the wrong brand name. The page says &#x27;Submit the form&#x27; but no form is visible, only an &#x27;EMAIL ME&#x27; button. 
 2 Why that costs leads An investor or buyer who lands here cannot see a single home, price or photo of anything Brent Nolan actually has for sale — so there is nothing to compare, shortlist or get excited about. The wrong brand name (&#x27;Apex&#x27;) and stock photos make the brokerage look unfinished, and with no form, a visitor who is ready to ask about a home has to leave the site to email. Every one of those visitors can simply go to Zillow instead. 
 3 How this redesign fixes it Real listings with photos, prices and key facts sit at the top of the page, searchable by price, beds, baths and type. The correct brand name appears throughout, every photo is labelled concept imagery, and a short lead form captures name, email and what the visitor is looking for — so interest turns into a conversation instead of a bounce.
