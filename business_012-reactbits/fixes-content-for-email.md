What this redesign fixes 
 A straight comparison: what is missing on the brokerage's current website, why that costs enquiries from its own clients, and how this concept fixes it. No sales figures are claimed — none were available. 

 1 What&#x27;s missing on the real site The homepage is text-only, with no listings, no listing photos, no search or filter, and no lead-capture form. The only image is a screenshot, and the homepage &#x27;Contact Us / Email Address&#x27; section is truncated, with no email shown at all. 
 2 Why that costs leads Paris buyers are often shopping on a tight budget — they need to see price first, then the home. A text-only page gives them neither: no $82,500 starter home, no $168,500 renovated home, no photo to fall for. With the email address cut off and no form, even a buyer who likes what they read has no easy way to reach out. They move on to a site that shows homes. 
 3 How this redesign fixes it Every real listing gets a photo, a price and its key facts, searchable by price, beds, baths and type — so a budget buyer can find the homes they can afford in seconds. The phone number and email are visible in the header, on every listing and in the footer, and a short form captures enquiries with the property attached.
